Court Watch starts from every tennis court mapped in Ohio and follows each one through every year of public aerial imagery: still tennis, painted over for pickleball (a hybrid), fully converted, or gone.

How this works, and what to trust

Method

  • Where the courts are. Every sport=tennis, pickleball or padel pitch in OpenStreetMap. Banks of courts are split into individual footprints by standard court dimensions; pickleball courts painted on a former tennis court are folded back into that court's footprint.
  • What they looked like. USDA NAIP aerial imagery (0.6 to 1 m per pixel), flown over Ohio every two to three years, via Microsoft's Planetary Computer.
  • What class each court is. Each footprint is cut out of each year's image and classified by a small neural network trained on a few hundred hand-labelled examples. Where it cannot see the court (trees, shadow, clouds) it says so instead of guessing.
  • What changed. Classes are compared year to year per footprint. Reversals (pickleball back to tennis) and low-confidence steps are flagged for human review rather than counted silently.

Caveats

  • Courts not in OpenStreetMap are invisible here. That undercount is real and not estimated.
  • Pickleball lines are a few centimetres wide; at 0.6 m per pixel they are visible as faint contrast, not as lines. Some hybrids will be missed and some worn tennis courts will be called hybrid. Flagged courts are exactly the ones a person should look at.
  • Where a footprint was converted to pickleball but OpenStreetMap has not mapped the new courts, the number of pickleball courts on it is reported as unknown, never guessed.
  • Numbers on this page are model output plus reviewer corrections; every court's history and confidence is in the download.
